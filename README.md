# HR-Analytics



**HR Analytics Dashboard – Understanding Employee Attrition & Workforce Trends**

An interactive HR Analytics Dashboard built using Power BI to explore employee data and understand workforce trends. The dashboard focuses on employee attrition, demographics, department-wise turnover, job satisfaction, and educational backgrounds.

The main aim of this project is to turn raw HR data into meaningful insights that can help organizations understand why employees leave and where improvements in employee retention may be needed.

## 1.Purpose

Employee attrition is one of the common challenges faced by organizations. Losing employees can affect productivity, increase recruitment costs, and create additional pressure on existing teams.

To better understand this challenge, I created this HR Analytics Dashboard using Power BI. It provides a clear overview of the workforce through interactive charts, KPIs, and filters.

The dashboard helps explore questions such as which departments have the highest attrition, which age groups experience more employee turnover, how attrition varies across job roles, and how job satisfaction is distributed among employees.

This project helped me explore how data visualization can make HR data easier to understand and support better workforce-related decisions.

## 2. Tech Stack

The following tools and technologies were used in this project:

- 📊 **Power BI Desktop** – Used to build the dashboard and create interactive visualizations.
- 📂 **Power Query** – Used for data cleaning, transformation, and preparation.
- 🧠 **DAX (Data Analysis Expressions)** – Used to create calculated measures and important HR metrics such as total attrition, active employees, and attrition rate.
- 📈 **Data Visualization** – Used different charts, including bar charts, column charts, line charts, donut charts, and tables to present insights.
- 🎛️ **Interactive Slicers** – Added filters for gender, marital status, business travel, and education level to make the dashboard interactive.
- 📁 **File Formats** – `.pbix` for the Power BI project and `.png` for the dashboard preview.

## 3. Data Source

**Source: AI-Generated Synthetic HR Employee Dataset**

For this project, I used an AI-generated synthetic HR dataset containing employee-related information. The data was created to simulate a realistic workplace environment so that I could explore different HR scenarios and perform meaningful analysis.

The dataset includes information about employee demographics, attrition, education, departments, job roles, business travel, monthly income, and job satisfaction.

Some of the key attributes included in the dataset are:

- Employee Age and Gender
- Employee Attrition Status
- Department and Job Role
- Education and Education Field
- Marital Status
- Business Travel Frequency
- Monthly Income
- Job Satisfaction Rating

## 4. Business Problem

Employee attrition can be difficult to manage when organizations don't have a clear understanding of their workforce trends.

Simply knowing how many employees have left is not enough. HR teams also need to understand which departments are affected the most, which job roles experience higher turnover, and how employee demographics relate to attrition.

Without proper analysis, identifying these patterns can be time-consuming.

Through this dashboard, I wanted to explore employee data from different perspectives and make important HR insights easier to identify.

### • Goal of the Dashboard

The main goal was to create an interactive dashboard that provides a complete overview of employee attrition and workforce distribution.

The dashboard aims to:

- Track important HR metrics in one place.
- Understand employee attrition across different age groups and genders.
- Identify departments and job roles with higher employee turnover.
- Explore employee satisfaction across different job roles.
- Analyze attrition based on educational background.
- Allow users to interact with the data through multiple filters.
- Present HR information in a simple and visually understandable way.

### • Key Insights & Business Impact

After exploring the dashboard, a few important observations stand out:

- **Departmental Attrition:** R&D contributes the largest share of total attrition, accounting for 56.12% of employee exits.
- **Job Role Analysis:** Laboratory Technicians have the highest attrition among the listed job roles, followed by Sales Executives and Research Scientists.
- **Workforce Distribution:** The 25–34 age group represents a significant portion of the workforce.
- **Employee Retention:** The overall attrition rate is 16.12%, making employee retention an important area to investigate.
- **Education Analysis:** Life Sciences and Medical backgrounds account for a large share of employee attrition.

These insights can help HR teams identify areas that may need closer attention.

From a business perspective, this type of analysis can support better employee retention planning, workforce management, employee engagement, and HR decision-making.

  ### Key Recommendations

Based on the dashboard findings, here are a few areas the HR team could investigate:

- **Focus on R&D and Sales:** These departments account for the largest share of employee attrition. Investigate department-specific factors that may contribute to employee turnover.
- **Review High-Attrition Job Roles:** Laboratory Technicians and Sales Executives show comparatively high attrition counts. Explore workload, compensation, career growth, and job satisfaction in these roles.

- **Improve Employee Satisfaction:** Review job satisfaction levels across roles to identify areas where workplace improvements may help.
- **Investigate Education-Field Trends:** Explore why employees from certain educational backgrounds account for higher attrition counts.
